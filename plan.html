Rubric Design Fundamentals

A practical learning sequence for tertiary teaching academics, learning designers, tutors, markers and academic staff.

Current delivery: chapters 1–7. Approximately 73 minutes, including practice. All seven chapters are available. Full-book estimate: 73 minutes.

The learning journey

Diagnose vague differentiation, develop a language for observable quality, then design and review a usable rubric. Each chapter combines a visual explanation, a decision with feedback and a task to take back to practice.

Workshop conventions

A dark green workshop board, warm ivory type and restrained colour: amber for a label or assumption to inspect, blue for student action, mint for explained relationships and useful revisions. Animated changes follow Australian English narration. Captions, keyboard controls, reduced motion and readable companions support different ways of working.

Feedback explains the evidence behind an answer. The writing tasks are unscored, and notes can be downloaded. Chapter 2 uses an illustrative inquiry mixing panel to explore task-specific emphasis, not numerical scoring weights. Chapter 3 uses response structures to support judgement in context.

Editorial decisions

The source enumerates seven topics despite describing six. This plan uses seven. Shared descriptors support judgement and calibration but cannot guarantee identical grades.

Chapter 3 teaches SOLO without automatically converting its levels to grades. Current UQ requirements were checked on 10 October 2026: supplementary eligibility is conditional, and the source’s unverified distribution thresholds are omitted. Consult the current UQ Assessment Procedure and your course requirements. Chapter 2 retains the guide’s qualification that Values of Inquiry has not been independently validated specifically for rubric descriptor writing.
